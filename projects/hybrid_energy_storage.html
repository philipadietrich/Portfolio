Hybrid Mechanical Energy Storage System

This project explores a hybrid mechanical energy storage concept that combines multiple storage and recovery methods into a single integrated system.

Project Overview

This system combines compressed air and pumped hydropower to store and recover energy. The goal was to build a proof of concept, exploring if these mechanical energy storage methods could hypothetically support the energy needs of a home or apartment complex.

My Role

I was responsible for the hydropower subsystem, researching several water turbines and testing an initial option before selecting a Pelton turbine for the final design. I developed the subsystem layout, selected reservoirs, hoses, fittings, and seals, and designed the nozzle. I also established key design parameters and created schematics for the final turbine setup.

Skills and Tools Used

SolidWorks, Fluid Mechanics, Energy Analysis, Mechanical Design

What I Learned

This project taught me that the best option on paper is not always the most practical to build. Although the crossflow turbine offered several advantages, sealing around the shaft proved too difficult for our design. The Pelton turbine allowed us to connect to the shared drive shaft without the same sealing challenges, making it a more practical choice. This experience strengthened my understanding of mechanical design trade-offs and the importance of balancing theoretical performance with what we could realistically build and implement.
